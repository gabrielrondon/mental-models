# Sunk Cost Fallacy

> "When you find yourself in a hole, stop digging." — Will Rogers

---

## 1. Core Intuition & Mechanism

Suppose you buy a non-refundable \$100 ticket to an outdoor concert. On the day of the show, a violent sleet storm erupts, the temperature plummets to freezing, you develop a fever, and you dread the thought of standing in the icy rain for four hours.

Yet you catch yourself saying: *"I spent \$100 on this ticket; I have to go, otherwise my money was wasted!"*

This is the **Sunk Cost Fallacy** in its purest form.
Consider the true reality:
- The \$100 is **gone forever**. It was spent in the past and cannot be recovered whether you attend or stay home.
- Your choice now is not between "wasting \$100" and "getting your money's worth."
- Your real choice is between:
  1. Being miserable and sick in freezing sleet (- enjoyment, - health).
  2. Staying warm at home with hot soup (+ comfort, + recovery).

By going to the concert, you don't recover your \$100; you simply compound your financial loss with a physical and emotional loss!

In economics, rational decision-making considers **only forward-looking marginal costs and marginal benefits**. The past is an unchangeable sunk variable.

---

## 2. When to Apply (Diagnostic Checklist)

Use this checklist during roadmap reviews, product sunsets, and budget meetings:

- [ ] **If we had zero historic investment in this project today, would we fund it with our remaining cash?**
- [ ] **Are we justifying ongoing expenditures with phrases like *"We have come too far to turn back now"*?**
- [ ] **Is the primary fear stopping us from killing this initiative the personal embarrassment of admitting failure?**
- [ ] **What is the Opportunity Cost of continuing to throw engineers and capital at this sunk asset?**
- [ ] **Have we clearly distinguished between healthy perseverance through friction and irrational stubbornness in a dead end?**

## 3. Real-World Case Studies

### Technology & Engineering
*The Concorde Supersonic Transport:* The British and French governments continued financing the supersonic Concorde aircraft for decades long after economic models proved the plane would never be commercially viable due to fuel inefficiency and sonic boom restrictions. Because both governments had already sunk hundreds of millions of pounds and national prestige into development, neither side was politically willing to cancel the project. It became the textbook geopolitical sunk-cost disaster (the "Concorde Effect").

### Business & Strategy
*The \$20 Million Failed ERP Migration:* An enterprise initiates an SAP/Oracle migration budgeted at \$5 million over 12 months. Two years later, \$15 million has been spent, and the system fails basic inventory audits. Instead of cutting losses, management approves another \$10 million because "we cannot throw away the \$15 million already spent." Six months later, the project is abandoned completely, having destroyed \$25 million.

### Everyday High-Stakes Decisions
*Finishing Bad Books and Movies:* Forcing yourself to sit through the remaining 300 pages of a boring, poorly written book solely because you spent \$20 on the paperback and read the first 100 pages. Your life is finite; throwing away four hours of precious cognitive time does not refund the \$20.

## 4. Failure Modes & Cognitive Blindspots

- **Confusing Sunk Costs with Valid Option Value:** Sometimes past investment has created valuable foundational infrastructure, data, or learning that dramatically lowers the *forward* cost of finishing. If the forward marginal return exceeds forward marginal cost, finishing is rational! Sunk cost fallacy applies only when the *forward* investment is also negative EV.
- **The Quitter's Fallacy (Lack of Grit):** Abandoning hard, worthwhile initiatives at the first sign of normal resistance under the false pretense that "this is a sunk cost."

---

## 5. The Latticework (Related Models)

- **[[Opportunity Cost]]**: The ultimate antidote. Calculate what new, thriving project that capital could build today.
- **[[Loss Aversion]]**: The psychological engine of the fallacy: humans despise the pain of locking in a loss.
- **[[Inversion]]**: Invert the question: what happens if we continue feeding this sinkhole for another two years?

## 6. AI Agent Reasoning Protocol

When an AI agent is evaluating project cancellation or refactoring:

```text
[STEP 1: ZERO OUT HISTORICAL CAPITAL]
Erase past hours, commits, and capital from the decision spreadsheet. Treat balance as \$0.

[STEP 2: MODEL FORWARD MARGINAL VALUE]
Estimate the remaining cost to completion versus the realistic future benefit discounted to present value.

[STEP 3: COMPARE AGAINST FORWARD ALTERNATIVES]
Compare the forward ROI of completing the project against deploying those same remaining resources to other backlog initiatives.

[STEP 4: ISSUE UNBIASED VERDICT]
If forward EV is negative or beaten by alternatives, recommend immediate termination with a blameless retrospective.
```
